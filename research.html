TÜBİTAK 2204-A · High school research project

Production of Hernia Meshes: Polyurethane Acrylate by UV Polymerisation and Antimicrobial Coating with Benzalkonium Chloride

Hernia meshPolyurethane acrylateUV polymerisationBenzalkonium chlorideAntimicrobial coating

Abstract

This project develops porous hernia-mesh prototypes from biocompatible polyurethane acrylate (PUA) using UV polymerisation and templating. UV-curable PUA allows a controlled pore structure, tunable mechanical strength and the biocompatibility needed for surgical implants. The templating approach controls pore size and distribution, so the mesh can support tissue integration.

To reduce the risk of infection during surgery, the mesh surface is coated with the antimicrobial agent benzalkonium chloride (BAC) by dip coating, aiming for a uniform, stable layer with lasting activity. Antimicrobial performance is evaluated by culturing the meshes on two safe growth media, Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA) and Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA). The overall aim is a usable prototype that is biocompatible, safe and made with an innovative, repeatable method.

Method at a glance

1
Make the meshCure PUA with UV light over a template to form a porous structure.
2
Coat the surfaceDip-coat the prototypes with benzalkonium chloride for antimicrobial activity.
3
Test with culturesCulture the meshes on TSA and SDA media and observe microbial growth.

Why it matters

Hernia repair commonly uses an implanted mesh, and infection at the implant is a real risk. A mesh that is both porous enough to integrate with tissue and resistant to microbes could make these operations safer. This project explores whether UV-cured PUA can meet both needs.
